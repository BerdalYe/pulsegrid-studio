A free and open-source music studio

PulseGrid Studio has a step sequencer, piano roll, playlist and mixer, with 150 instruments and 14 effects built in. It runs on Windows, Mac and in your browser, and its source code is on GitHub under the GPL.

The workflow producers already know: patterns in the channel rack, melodies in the piano roll, the song in the playlist, and the mix in the mixer.

01

Program drums in seconds

Click or drag across the step grid to lay down a beat. Set velocity per step, add swing, and give every channel its own volume, pan and mixer insert.

  • Ten recorded drum kits and nine drum-machine voices
  • Hear a sound in the browser, then drag it onto the rack
  • Paint by dragging, right-drag a step for velocity
02

Write melodies in the piano roll

Draw, paint, erase and select notes, then quantise them to the grid. Scale highlighting keeps you in key; ghost notes show what the other channels play.

  • Velocity lane under every note
  • Copy, paste and duplicate with the usual shortcuts
  • Record straight in from a MIDI keyboard
03

Mix with real routing

Sixteen mixer channels, each with its own effect chain. Send them to a shared reverb, group them into buses, and watch peak meters with clip lights.

  • 14 effects, from EQ and compressor to phaser and bitcrusher
  • Sends and bus routing, with loop protection
  • Master limiter so exports never clip
04

See the whole song at a glance

Every clip on the playlist shows what's inside it: drum hits laid out in rows, melodies by pitch, audio as a waveform. Arrange sections, drop in markers and loop the part you're working on.

  • Patterns, audio and automation on colour-coded lanes
  • Section markers and loop regions
  • Drag to move, pull an edge to resize
05

Take your projects anywhere

Sign in with a free account and choose which projects go to your cloud. Those follow you between Windows, Mac and the web studio, uploading after every save. The rest stay on your computer, and nothing leaves it until you choose.

VST3 instruments play inside PulseGrid (Windows).

  • VST3 instruments. PulseGrid finds the VST3 instruments installed on your PC and lists them under VST3 plugins in the browser and the Add menu. Add one and it gets a channel like any other: play it from the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard, write notes for it in the piano roll, and mix it in the mixer. The Akai Studio Instrument Collection that comes with the MPK mini IV works this way, with no MIDI cable or second program.
  • The plugin's own window. Click the channel's synth button, or press F8, to open the plugin's window. It stays above the studio while you use it and hides when you minimise the studio.
  • Saved with your project and in your exports. The sound you pick in a plugin is saved with the project and comes back when you open it. Exported WAV and MP3 files include plugin channels.
  • Plugins run on their own. Plugins run in a separate program, so a plugin that crashes can't take the studio down with it: PulseGrid tells you and starts the plugins again.
  • Settings → Plugins lists the plugins found, lets you add folders to search, and searches again after you install something new.
  • VST3 plugins are for Windows for now, and only instruments; effect plugins in the mixer come later.

PulseGrid Studio is now open source. Also: 150 instruments with drum kits you can build a beat from, a new playlist, a browser and master monitor in the style of FL Studio, a Settings window, and MIDI out for the instruments that came with your keyboard.

  • 150 instruments, sorted by family. Drums, Keys, Mallets & Bells, Guitars, Bass, Strings, Brass & Winds, Voices, Synths, Pads and FX. The desktop apps add recorded instruments: pianos, organs, guitars, basses, a full orchestra, choirs and ten drum kits including an 808/909 kit. They come with the app, work offline, and are saved with your project and included when you export audio.
  • Open source. The source code is on GitHub under the GNU GPL, version 3: read it, build it, change it and share your changes. Bug reports and pull requests are welcome.
  • Drum kits you can build a beat with. Each kit has its own folder: the Drum Set adds its kick, snare, clap and hats as separate channels, so you click a beat in on the channel rack the way you do in FL; the Full Kit puts every drum on one channel, with each key of the piano roll named after its drum (on the computer keyboard, Z is the kick); and each drum is there on its own too. A kit's panel has 16 pads to hear it, or to change which drum a channel plays, and a list of kits to swap to.
  • A new playlist. Tools like FL's (draw, delete and mute, so a clip can be silenced without removing it), a strip showing the whole song that you click or drag to move around, a ruler you drag to move the playhead, a zoom readout and a button that fits the whole song in the window. Tracks have a mute light, solo and a menu (colour, height, move, clear, delete; or right-click the track). Drag a pattern from the list onto a lane to place it, and double-click a clip to open its pattern.
  • A piano roll that shows what's playing. Keys light up as their notes play, and as you play them on the computer keyboard or a MIDI keyboard; the notes under the playhead glow; the key of the row under your mouse is marked. Click and hold a key to hear it, and slide along the keys to play them in turn.
  • Touchpads. In the piano roll and the playlist, two fingers scroll in any direction and a pinch zooms. A mouse wheel still zooms, as before.
  • FX for builds and drops. Noise riser, downlifter, impact, sub drop, reverse crash and white noise sweep, plus reverse cymbal, orchestra hit and atmospheres.
  • Hear it first, then add it. In the browser, a click plays an instrument or a sample so you can hear it; drag it onto the channel rack to add it. Drop a sample on a sample channel to swap its sound. You can still add from the channel rack and the Add menu. Imported and recorded audio goes into the sample list.
  • A browser in the style of FL Studio. A row of icons across the top switches between Projects, Instruments, Samples, Keyboard & MIDI, Cloud sync and Demo projects. The instrument library is a searchable tree. Drag the browser's edge to make it wider.
  • A header laid out like FL Studio's. The top row holds the menus, pattern and song mode, the transport, tempo and time, and a master monitor: an oscilloscope, a stereo peak meter with peak hold and a clip light, and a performance box with CPU load and its recent history, memory in use, the number of voices playing, and a warning if the sound drops out. The second row holds the hint panel, with the project's name and a hint for whatever you point at, then the windows, snap, the pattern and the master knobs.
  • Choose what goes to the cloud, and delete projects. Only the projects you choose go to your cloud now: the cloud button beside a project in the Projects tab saves it there, open or not. The bin button deletes a project to the Recycle Bin, so you can get it back.
  • A Settings window. Press F10 or choose Options → Settings: the audio output and latency, metronome volume, how hard the computer keyboard plays, the MIDI port and drum-pad channel, the theme and the size of the whole interface (desktop), and more.
  • Panels that behave. Leave a panel partly out of view, or bring it near a side and it locks there. Resize from any edge or corner, double-click a title bar to maximise, and use the ▾ on each title bar for that panel's own menu. The channel rack's steps stretch to fill their panel, and its playhead now sits exactly over the playing step.
  • Plugins: Wavetable Synth, MIDI Out and Sampler. A recorded instrument's panel lists the instruments by family, so you can swap to another one, and loads .sf2 SoundFont files of your own. MIDI Out sends a channel's notes to another program, for example the Akai Studio Instrument Collection that comes with the MPK mini IV, through a virtual MIDI cable such as loopMIDI.
  • Better MIDI keyboard support. Keyboards with several ports, like the MPK mini IV, play without stray notes from their control ports, and a live MIDI monitor shows what each key, pad and knob sends.
  • Menus like a desktop program, with shortcuts in their own column and flyouts, opening just under the menu bar, and staying signed in when you close the app.
  • The mixer no longer writes "C" under every pan knob: a knob at the centre shows nothing, and one turned to a side shows how far (L20, R35).

Installing

  1. Open the installer (an .msi file). It installs just for you, with no administrator needed, adds shortcuts to your desktop and Start menu, and starts PulseGrid Studio.
  2. If Windows shows “Windows protected your PC”, choose More info → Run anyway. The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows doesn't recognise the publisher.
  3. If Smart App Control blocks it, Windows won't offer a way to run it until the app is code-signed. Use the web studio in the meantime; it's the same studio.
  4. Accept the user agreement the first time the studio opens.
  5. Had version 1.2 or earlier, installed with the old .exe? Remove it first under Settings → Apps. Your projects stay where they are. After that, updates arrive by themselves.

Installing on a Mac

  1. Open the .dmg file and drag PulseGrid Studio onto the Applications folder. One download works on both Intel and Apple Silicon Macs.
  2. The first time, open it with a right-click. In Applications, right-click (or Control-click) PulseGrid Studio, choose Open, then Open again. The app isn't signed with an Apple Developer certificate yet, so macOS says it can't verify who made it. After that it opens normally.
  3. If macOS says the app is “damaged”, open Terminal and run xattr -cr "/Applications/PulseGrid Studio.app", then open it again.
  4. New versions: the app tells you when one is out and takes you to this page to download it.
  5. On a Mac laptop, the F5–F9 window shortcuts need the Fn key held, or turn on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in System Settings → Keyboard.

Frequently asked

Is PulseGrid Studio really free?

Yes. The desktop app and the web studio are free, with no subscription, ads or in-app purchases, and you never need an account. An account is only for the optional cloud sync.

Is it open source?

Yes. The source code is on GitHub under the GNU General Public License, version 3. You can build the app yourself, change it and share your version, as long as you share its source code under the same licence. The licence covers the app, not your music: what you make with it is yours.

Who owns the music I make?

You do. Everything you make with PulseGrid Studio is yours, including for commercial releases, and the built-in sounds are free to use in it. PulseGrid claims no rights to your music and you don't need to credit it. Read the short user agreement in English or Norwegian.

Where are my projects saved?

In the desktop app, in Documents\PulseGrid Studio by default. Change it under File → Change save folder…. In the web studio, in your browser's own storage on your device; use File → Download project to keep a copy as a file. With a free account you can also keep the projects you choose in the cloud and open them on any of your computers. To delete a project, use the bin button beside it in the browser's Projects tab; it goes to the Recycle Bin.

How does cloud sync work?

Create a free account under Cloud sync in the studio's browser. Then choose which projects go to your cloud: the cloud button beside a project in the Projects tab saves it there, and from then on it uploads after each save. The same account on another computer lists it under Cloud sync, ready to open. If the same project was changed on two computers, neither change is thrown away: the other version is kept as its own project, named “(other version)”. You can stop syncing a project, or remove it from the cloud, at any time. The privacy notice says exactly what is stored.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything works without one, including saving, exporting and the web studio. An account is only needed if you want your projects synced between computers. Until you sign in, the studio never contacts the cloud.

How do updates work?

The Windows app checks for a new version shortly after it starts and every few hours while it's open. When there is one, it downloads in the background, is checked against its checksum, and a Restart to update button appears in the menu bar; your project is saved first, then the new version installs itself and opens. The Mac app checks too, and tells you when a new version is ready to download. You can turn the check off in Settings (F10). The web studio is always the latest version.

What keyboard shortcuts are there?

Press F1 in the studio (or choose Help → Keyboard shortcuts) for the full list. A few to start with: Space plays and pauses, Enter plays from the beginning, L turns looping on and off, K is the metronome, F5–F9 open the windows, and the scroll wheel zooms the piano roll and playlist. The keys that play notes (Z to M and Q to I) are left alone, so shortcuts on those letters use Ctrl or Alt.

Does my MIDI keyboard work?

Any class-compliant USB MIDI keyboard works, including the Akai MPK mini IV. Keys play the selected instrument, pads play your drum channels (on MIDI channel 10, or the one you set in Settings → MIDI), and the joystick handles pitch bend and modulation. If a knob doesn't do what you expect, right-click any knob on screen and turn the one on your keyboard to link them. The MIDI monitor in the browser's Keyboard & MIDI tab shows what each key, pad and knob sends.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows doesn't recognise the publisher. SmartScreen shows a warning you can pass: check that the SHA-256 checksum above matches your download, then choose More info → Run anyway. On Windows 11 PCs with Smart App Control turned on, unsigned apps are blocked with no way to run them. Use the web studio there until a signed release is available.

Does PulseGrid collect any data?

No. There is no analytics, advertising or tracking, and your projects never leave your device unless you send them somewhere. Without an account, the desktop app's only network request is its update check, which you can switch off. If you choose to sign in for cloud sync, your synced projects are stored with Supabase. See the privacy notice.

How do I add an instrument?

Open the Instruments tab in the browser on the left. Click an instrument to hear it, and drag the one you want onto the channel rack. You can also pick one from the channel rack's Add list, or from the Add menu. Samples work the same way in the Samples tab.

What's the best way to make a beat with a drum kit?

Open Drums in the Instruments tab, open a kit and drag its Drum Set onto the channel rack. You get its kick, snare, clap and hats on channels of their own: click their steps to make the beat. Need a tom or a crash too? Drag that drum from the same folder. To play a kit from a keyboard or pad controller instead, use its Full Kit: every key is a different drum, and the piano roll names them.

Can I use the instruments that came with my keyboard?

On Windows, yes: VST3 instruments, such as the Akai Studio Instrument Collection, play inside PulseGrid. Install them as usual, then find them under VST3 plugins in the browser or the Add menu. Some plugins must be activated in their maker's app first, or they stay silent. On a Mac, add MIDI Out, pick a virtual MIDI cable (the IAC Driver), and play the instrument in a program that hosts plugins; its sound then comes from that program, so it isn't part of a PulseGrid export.

Can I move projects between the web studio and the desktop app?

Yes. Both use .pulsegrid project files: download a project from one and open it in the other.
